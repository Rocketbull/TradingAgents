# Monthly Investor Commentary - June 2026

For the latest completed portfolio period, from 2026-05-27 to 2026-06-26, the portfolio returned +4.94% compared with -2.61% for SPY. Active return was +7.55%. Since inception in this corrected backtest run, total return stood at +349.32%, with a Sharpe ratio of 1.71 and a maximum drawdown of -12.65%.

The portfolio benefited from strong exposure to AI infrastructure, semiconductors, memory, and data-storage companies. This positioning was meaningfully different from the broad market and was the main driver of outperformance during a period when SPY declined. The strongest post-rebalance holdings included SanDisk, Micron, Intel, AMD, Nvidia, Marvell, Western Digital, Dell, Seagate, and Palo Alto Networks.

The June rebalance kept the portfolio focused on technology leadership while rotating within that theme. The portfolio added exposure to names including AbbVie, Applied Materials, Datadog, Johnson & Johnson, Intel, Citi, Corning, UnitedHealth, Robinhood, and Micron. It reduced exposure to Apple, Alphabet, Tesla, Lumentum, ConocoPhillips, Cisco, Verizon, Nvidia, Microsoft, and GE Vernova. The result is still a growth-oriented portfolio, but with some modest broadening beyond the highest-profile mega-cap technology names.

The market backdrop was mixed. Broad equity performance was weaker, while AI-linked hardware and memory companies continued to attract investor attention after strong earnings and demand commentary from the memory sector. Inflation and interest-rate risk also remained relevant in late June, with reported inflation still elevated and Federal Reserve policy staying restrictive. This combination created a market where broad index returns were soft, but selected technology supply-chain winners continued to perform well.

Current market-risk readings from the SPY dashboard were not signaling broad-market stress as of 2026-06-26. SPY was down on the day, but breadth and macro conditions did not indicate an elevated broad-market downside warning. That said, the portfolio's main risk is concentration: recent gains are tied heavily to memory, semiconductors, storage, and AI infrastructure. If investor confidence in AI spending, memory pricing, or chip margins weakens, active returns could reverse quickly.

Overall, June was a strong relative month. The portfolio outperformed by owning the market's strongest leadership group while SPY itself was negative. The next several weeks should be watched for whether technology leadership continues to broaden or begins to narrow further, and whether inflation and rate concerns start to pressure growth valuations.
